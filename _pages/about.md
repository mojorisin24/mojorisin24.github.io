I am a cognitive and computational neuroscientist who studies attention, working memory, and decision-making with human EEG (and fMRI), behavioral experiments, and modern computational methods. I work on how the mind prioritizes rare, salient, and behaviorally relevant information, and I use those same signals and methods to study the cognitive changes tied to risk for psychosis.

I earned my **Ph.D. in Neuroscience from UC Davis (2024)** with Dr. Steven J. Luck at the Center for Mind and Brain, where I ran EEG/ERP studies of attention and working memory and built multivariate decoding and dimensionality-reduction tools now used across the lab. I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar with Dr. Daniel Mathalon at **UCSF and the San Francisco VA**, where I apply multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) to large-scale EEG and fMRI data from the **AMP-SCZ** and **NAPLS** consortia to identify electrophysiological markers of psychosis risk.

A through-line in my work is getting more out of human data: developing rigorous, reproducible methods and applying them across core cognition and clinical translation.

Outside the lab, I enjoy exploring the outdoors, playing guitar, and spending time with friends. Feel free to reach out at [Carlos.Carrasco@ucsf.edu](mailto:Carlos.Carrasco@ucsf.edu).
