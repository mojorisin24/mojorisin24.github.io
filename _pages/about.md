I am a computational neuroscientist focused on **biomarker discovery** — building machine-learning and multivariate methods that turn high-dimensional neural data into reliable, scalable signals for earlier detection and better treatment of CNS disorders. I care about the full path from raw signal to validated, reproducible biomarker: the kind of translational work that moves a candidate from the lab toward the clinic.

My current research applies **machine learning, multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA), and principal component analysis (PCA)** to EEG/ERP data to characterize neural processing in individuals at clinical high risk for psychosis. The goal is practical: improve biomarker fidelity and develop reliable, deployable tools for earlier detection and intervention in schizophrenia. This work sits at the intersection of computational method development, clinical populations, and reproducible analysis pipelines — the same intersection that drives biomarker programs in pharma and biotech.

I earned my **PhD in Neuroscience from UC Davis (2024)**, training with Dr. Steven J. Luck at the Center for Mind and Brain, where I combined experimental and computational approaches — including machine learning on electrophysiological data — to study working memory, selective attention, learning, and decision-making. I currently work as a Postdoctoral Scholar in the Brain Imaging and EEG Lab at UCSF and the San Francisco VA, with Dr. Daniel Mathalon, MD PhD. My published work spans both basic cognitive neuroscience and EEG methodology.

**What I bring to a biomarker or translational-neuroscience team:**

- **Machine learning & multivariate methods** — MVPA, PCA, and classification/decoding applied to noisy, high-dimensional neural data.
- **Biomarker discovery & validation** — a focus on reliability, reproducibility, and building measures that hold up outside a single dataset.
- **Clinical & translational orientation** — direct experience with clinical high-risk populations and the questions that matter for early detection and intervention.
- **End-to-end EEG/neuroimaging pipelines** — signal processing, feature extraction, and reproducible analysis workflows.

I am currently open to computational neuroscience, biomarker, and data science roles in industry (biotech/pharma). If you're working on translational CNS biomarkers or ML for neural data, I'd love to talk — reach me at [Carlos.Carrasco@ucsf.edu](mailto:Carlos.Carrasco@ucsf.edu).

Outside of the lab, I enjoy exploring the outdoors, playing guitar, and spending time with friends.
